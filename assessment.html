Petrilab/ Assessment Layer/ Committee Position/ 23 Aug 2026

Don't build a testing product. Build the ledger that testing products write into.

You have made teaching cost 30 seconds. If assessment costs thirty minutes, teachers will not do it, no evidence will exist, and everything downstream — the reports, the parent promise, the consistency claim — becomes a hypothesis nobody can falsify. This is the committee's position on what the assessment layer must be, and on what it takes to make other applications want to connect to it.

Verdict
Ledger, not gradebook

In 2026 anyone can generate a question. Almost nobody has an address to write the answer to.

Your asset is not an item bank — item banks are commodities. It is PO-MATH-PREP-C-1.1-T2-D: a stable, framework-anchored, book-agnostic address that an observation can be written to and accumulated against for years. Own the address space and the record written on it, and every other application becomes a peripheral.

§ 1

The committee sits

Each member was asked the same question: what breaks first when a teacher who spends 30 seconds launching a lesson is asked to assess it? They disagree about the product. They agree about the failure.

Child psychologist · ages 8–11

At this age a single item measures the occasion, not the child.

Attention, hunger, seating, misreading the question — the noise floor at Preparatory is high enough that one item tells you almost nothing. Reliability comes from repetition across occasions, not from longer papers. And a test-shaped event contaminates its own measurement: anxiety depresses performance, so a nervous child's score measures composure.

The second claim is the useful one. A right answer is weak evidence — it is equally consistent with understanding, guessing, mimicry and copying. A patterned wrong answer is a window. "9,990 is bigger than 49,014 because 9 is bigger than 4" is a stable, nameable, teachable belief.

  • No single observation may change a verdict — the two-occasion rule.
  • No test day. Evidence is a byproduct of practice, never an event.
  • The child never sees a level or a rank. Only the next move.
  • At this stage knowledge is enactive — you can never ask what a child knows, only infer it from what they did and how they were wrong.
Educator · 20 years, mid-tier private

Every assessment product dies in week three, and never because the insight was bad.

It dies because it added an obligation. The ones that survive replace work the teacher was already doing. She already runs a class test, checks notebooks, corrects homework and asks "hands up who got it." Four assessment instruments, already in the timetable, producing no data.

And do not automate her judgement away. It is the highest-quality sensor in the room and the only reason she will believe the output. Ration it, don't replace it — spend it only where the machine is unsure.

  • Intercept, don't add. Instrument what already happens.
  • The wedge is marking, not analytics. "You stop marking 40 notebooks" sells. "You get a dashboard" does not.
  • Her override is sovereign, visible, and learned from — that is where trust is built.
  • Close the loop at what she must produce anyway: report-card comments and the parent meeting.
  • Warning: the day this data touches teacher appraisal, honest data stops arriving.
Parent · the one paying

No parent has ever wanted a score. They want to know whether their child is okay, and what to do tonight.

A percentage answers neither. What buys belief is specificity: "she still reads 4,072 as four hundred seventy-two" lands in a way no dashboard does, because it could only have come from watching my child.

The comparison instinct is not defeatable. Refuse a benchmark and parents substitute a worse one — the neighbour's child. Your stage model hands you the better frame for free: progress against a national competency, not a rank against classmates.

  • One named specific plus one ten-minute action. Nothing else.
  • Show the child's own work as the proof.
  • Frame progress stage-relative — "on track for Preparatory" — never rank-relative.
  • Sequence matters: teacher trust first, parent surface second. A parent view that exposes teacher behaviour kills the teacher's honesty, and her honesty is the entire data supply.
Academic coordinator · the buyer

Schools don't buy brilliance. They buy consistency.

The nightmare is specific: 5A and 5C finished the term in different places, taught by different teachers, and nobody knew until the board exam. Comparability across sections is the product being purchased.

But a coordinator who mandates scoring gets compliance data, which is worth nothing. Give her coverage and confidence, which cannot be faked by ticking boxes.

  • Speak the school's currency — term marks, syllabus coverage, report cards — or be a second system that gets abandoned.
  • She will ask "what happens when we change books" and "who owns this data." Layer 2 and DPDP are the sales answers, and both must exist before this is sellable.
  • Never per-teacher rankings. Section-level coverage only.
Where four people who agree on nothing agree

All four reject the same object: the high-stakes, teacher-authored, end-of-unit paper test. What they each want instead is assessment that is continuous, nearly free, diagnostic rather than evaluative, and — the one everybody forgets — returned.

§ 2

Two reframes before anything gets built

Assessment is not a sixth step. It is a layer over steps 3 and 4.

The gold method already contains Abhyas (practice) and Prayog (experiment). A child doing Abhyas is already producing evidence — it is simply thrown away. Adding "Step 6 · Assess" would break the 30-second promise, manufacture exactly the test-shaped event the psychologist warned about, and create the new obligation the educator says kills products. Don't add a step. Instrument the two you have.

The unit of the system is not a test, a score, or a report. It is an Observation.

This is the decision that makes an OS possible rather than a gradebook with an API bolted on. Everything upstream of the ledger — your app, a worksheet, a partner's practice game, a competitor's quiz engine — is merely an emitter. Everything downstream — the reteach card, the parent note, the coverage map, the report card — is a reader. The ledger is the only thing in the middle.

§ 3

The loop, and its time budget

Your teaching promise is a number, which is why it is credible. The assessment promise needs one too. The committee proposes the three-minute loop — per lesson, for a class of forty. Everything below is designed backwards from that budget, and the budget is the metric you should be most afraid of missing.

Step 1

Teacher — but she does not author

The lesson already carries its spine nodes from prep, so the check is pre-drafted the moment prep finishes. The Performance Outcome gives the task, the Knowledge Outcome gives the explanation, the misconception field gives the distractor. Layer 3 already promised this; assessment is the first thing that cashes it. Five or six items, blueprinted — every item's address known before any child answers.

Paper, board or device — the school's tier picks the render target, not the instrument. The same item object prints as a worksheet, casts to the TV as an oral check, or renders as taps. The evidence written to the ledger is identical either way. This is what keeps you medium-agnostic without building three products.

The machine auto-scores everything unambiguous and queues only what it is unsure about — one child, one call, one thumb-tap, with a default already selected. Her judgement enters the ledger as its own observation at the highest source weight. Override is sovereign, logged, and fed back into scoring.

Step 4
Return
Automatic — three audiences

Teacher: "six children still hold the leading-digit belief — here is a five-minute reteach," pushed into tomorrow's prep card. Parent: one named specific, one ten-minute action. Coordinator: coverage and confidence by section. If Return is ever manual, the loop is bookkeeping and it dies by week three.

Note what is not in the loop: authoring, marking, data entry, and report writing. Those are the four things you are removing. That is the pitch to a teacher — not analytics.

§ 4

The measurement model

  • States, not scores. Per learner × Performance Outcome: Not yet · Emerging · Secure, with an orthogonal flag for misconception held. A child can be Emerging and holding a misconception; those are different facts and different responses.
  • Confidence, not certainty. A function of observation count, independence (different occasions and different formats), recency, and source weight. Show it in the UI. A system that says "not sure yet" earns trust; one that is confidently wrong once never gets it back.
  • The two-occasion rule. A single observation can lower confidence and queue a re-check. It can never demote a child. This is a psychometric necessity at this age, and it also happens to make the product feel humane.
  • Decay. Confidence falls with time — March evidence is weak by August. This is honest measurement, and it manufactures precisely the right pedagogy: spaced retrieval checks. The measurement need and the learning science point the same way here, which is rare and worth exploiting.
  • Knowledge is inferred, never observed. A Knowledge Outcome has no item of its own. Its state is computed through PO_KO_Map: suspected when several dependent outcomes fail, diagnosed when its named misconception is matched twice. Which is why the misconception column is not decoration — it is the engine.
The line to put in front of a CPO

Diagnostic power lives in the distractor, not the answer. Scoring tells you a child was wrong; a matched misconception tells you what they believe instead — and belief is the only thing a teacher can actually act on tomorrow morning.

§ 5

Worked: one exit ticket, three predictions

Real identifiers from the workbook. The lesson is Class 5 comparing large numbers, addressed to PO-MATH-PREP-C-1.1-T2-D — "Compares 5- and 6-digit numbers and justifies the comparison using place value." Six items are generated, one of them carrying a distractor built from the misconception attached to a single concept.

Occasion 1 — Tuesday, written
Aarav circles 9,990 as greater than 49,014.
Nothing is concluded. One occasion, one format. Confidence on the node drops and a re-check is queued in a different format.
Occasion 2 — Thursday, oral warm-up
"Which is more — 9,990 or 49,014? How do you know?" He says 9,990. Because 9 is bigger than 4.
Two occasions, two formats, same pattern. The state changes: the misconception is now diagnosed, not suspected.
What the teacher spent
Roughly four seconds across two days — one tap Tuesday, one tap Thursday.
She authored nothing, marked nothing, and recorded nothing. The evidence was a byproduct of practice she was running anyway.

And now the graph pays. The diagnosed concept is a dependency of three performance outcomes across two threads — so the exit ticket did not score one lesson. It lowered belief across a competency and predicted two failures that have not happened yet.

One diagnosis, three affected outcomes. The teacher gets one reteach targeting one idea instead of three separate remediation topics appearing over three weeks. The parent gets one sentence. And if eleven of thirty-eight children hold the same belief, the coordinator is looking at a curriculum signal, not a child signal — comparison was taught before magnitude was secure.

§ 6

The OS: why anyone would connect

Start with the uncomfortable part. Standards with no traffic are dead. Nobody adopts an address space because it is elegant; they adopt it because the records already live there. So the sequence is not negotiable: win the classroom loop → publish the address space → open the write API → open the read API. Do not lead with the API, and do not say "OS" to a school. Say "the record."

The economics, said plainly

Every application sees only its own sessions. The ledger sees school and home and tuition and paper, across years, addressed to a national framework. A complete longitudinal picture of a child's learning can only exist in one place — and whoever holds it becomes the thing everyone else has to call. Each emitter makes the ledger more valuable to every reader, and each reader gives emitters a reason to write. The spine is the shared address space that makes the two sides commensurable at all.

Say the analogy, then disarm it

The economics here are a credit bureau's. The ethics must not be. The record is diagnostic and never ranking; it belongs to the child rather than the school; it is portable and erasable on request; it never scores a teacher; and it is never sold. Put that in the product, not the privacy policy — with children's data, the first breach of it ends the company.

Adopt standards, don't author one

You do not have the standing to publish a standard, and you don't need it. Every piece of plumbing already exists; your value goes in the payload, not the envelope.

The regulatory clock is a moat, if you start now

India's DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025 with enforcement from 13 May 2027 — roughly nine months out. Everyone under 18 is a child; verifiable parental consent is required; behavioural tracking and targeted advertising at children are banned outright; and the boundary between a school and an edtech platform is still unclear enough that the safe reading is the strict one. An assessment OS is, by definition, a children's-data business. Build consent scope, purpose limitation and erasure as ledger primitives — per-observation columns, not a policy page — and the deadline stops being a cost you absorb and becomes a wall competitors have to climb. Coordinators will start asking in 2027. Being able to answer in one sentence will win deals.

§ 8

Build order, with gates

Each phase is gated on evidence that the previous one worked. The gates matter more than the phases — they are what stop you shipping an API to nobody.

Phase 0
Instrument Abhyas — one subject, one competency

Generate → Judge → Return inside the existing lesson screen, for Preparatory Maths place value where the spine is complete. Ship the Observation schema on day one even though only one emitter writes to it. Everything after this depends on the schema being right, and nothing after this can fix it cheaply.

GateMedian judge-time under 120s, and ≥60% of taught nodes carrying at least one observation, after four weeks with real teachers.
Phase 1
The measurement engine

States, confidence, decay, the two-occasion rule, and KO inference through PO_KO_Map. This is where the fan-out in §5 becomes real rather than illustrative.

GateGraph predictions beat the teacher's own expectation on held-out items. If they don't, the spine's grain is wrong — and that is worth knowing before selling anything.
Phase 2
Return surfaces

Parent note, coordinator coverage map, report-card comment generator. Teacher surface first; parent surface only once teachers trust the data.

GateTeachers actually use the generated report-card comments at term end. That is the moment the data becomes load-bearing for them.
Phase 3
Publish the address space

Spine as a CASE-conformant framework, plus Layer 2 alignment for the two or three textbooks that actually matter in your schools. Not all books. The ones your schools use.

GateOne external partner maps their content to your nodes without your help. If they can't, the framework isn't published — it's just documented.
Phase 4
Open the ledger

Write API for emitters, then read API for readers, consent-scoped and DPDP-native from the first call. Placement is the hook that gets the first content partner to write.

GateShare of observations originating outside Petrilab. Below 10%, you are a classroom app with an API. Above 30%, you are the ledger.
§ 9

Instrument these, in this order

  • Judgement cost — median teacher seconds to close the loop per lesson. The survival metric. If it drifts past three minutes you have two weeks before logging stops.
  • Evidence density — observations per learner per week. Below roughly two, no state can reach confidence and the model has nothing to say.
  • Loop closure — share of flagged reteaches actually taught within seven days. The "does any of this change anything" metric, and the one investors will not think to ask about.
  • Node coverage and confidence — share of taught nodes observed, and share above threshold. This is the coordinator's product.
  • Diagnostic yield — misconceptions named per hundred observations. Tracks whether the catalogue is keeping up with the item generator.
  • Later, and only later — emitters connected, third-party share of observations, read-API calls.
