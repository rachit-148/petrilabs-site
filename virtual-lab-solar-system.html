Mission Control: tour the Solar System

You are a mission planner. Watch the planets race round the Sun, then land on each one and look up at its sky. Switch off the Sun, test which planet floats, and try to save the Earth from an asteroid.

What you will find out

How the planets move round the Sun, why far planets take so long, how every planet compares with Earth — and what the sky looks like from each of them.

How this lab works

  • 1In Planets, pick a planet and a speed — or move your finger round the Sun to turn time forwards yourself. The top picture shows the Solar System from above; the bottom one shows the sky from that planet.
  • 2Switch the top picture between Textbook view and True distances.
  • 3In Experiments, find your weight on other planets, switch off the Sun, fill a giant bathtub and save the Earth.
  • 4Answer three questions as you go, then finish with a summary.

For the teacher

Links to NCERT Class 6 Curiosity, Chapter 12 Beyond Earth (the Sun and planets, inner and outer planets, dwarf planets, asteroids, comets) and prepares for Class 7's Earth, Moon and the Sun. Every number in the lab is given compared with Earth, so students always have a reference.

In the Textbook view distances are squeezed and planets enlarged to fit the screen — switch to True distances and ask “why are the pictures in our books wrong?”. Save the Earth uses a simplified model to show one idea: a small push years early beats a big push at the last minute. Planet data from NASA fact sheets; moon counts as of March 2026.

The screen button on the left of the lab switches between the tall (phone) and wide (projector) layout. The Summary button opens the same write-up the class sees at the end.
