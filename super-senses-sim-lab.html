Who notices the rabbit first?

A rabbit is sitting still in the grass. An eagle, a dog and a bat are all nearby — and so are you. In this lab you move the rabbit closer and farther, change the light and the wind, and find out whose senses win.

What you will find out

Why every animal is a champion at one sense and ordinary at the others — and why that one sense is exactly the one it needs to stay alive.

How this lab works

  • 1Pick a sense at the top, pick an animal, then slide the rabbit closer or farther.
  • 2Watch both pictures — the field from above, and what that animal actually notices.
  • 3Answer three questions as you go. Each answer takes you to that exact situation.
  • 4Finish with a summary you can keep.

For the teacher

Project this on the board. The grey you mark on the detection bar is always the human score, so every reading is a direct comparison with the class's own senses.

Ask for a prediction before each slide: “How far away can a dog still smell the rabbit — the length of our classroom, or the length of the playground?” Then move the slider. The gap between the guess and 250 m is the lesson.

The screen button on the left of the lab switches between the tall (phone) and wide (projector) layout. The Why? button opens the teaching notes for the sense you are on.
