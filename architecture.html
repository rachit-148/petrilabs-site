§ 1

The four stages of school education

NCF-SE 2023 organises schooling into four stages rather than twelve grades. This matters structurally: a Competency is attained by the end of a stage, not by the end of a grade. Everything in the spine hangs off the stage, which is what lets the model stay independent of any particular school's grade-by-grade sequencing.

§ 2

Three layers: separating the stable from the disposable

The central design bet is that the messy part of curriculum work — this textbook teaches fractions in Grade 4, that one waits until Grade 5 — should never contaminate the model of learning itself. Splitting the system into three layers turns that mess from a content problem into a lookup.

§ 3

The flow-down, and where it forks

NCF describes a "flow-down" from Aims to Learning Outcomes, in which each level is derived from the one above it while covering it fully. The spine implements that chain — and then splits it in two at the Competency, because a Competency has both a performance face and a knowledge face.

§ 4

Performance Outcome vs Knowledge Outcome

This is the distinction the whole model turns on, and the one easiest to blur. The cleanest separator is grammatical: a Performance Outcome is verb-led with the learner as its subject; a Knowledge Outcome is a proposition with the idea as its subject.

What the learner does

Performance Outcome

An observable, assessable act. It sits on a developmental sequence and belongs to exactly one thread.

"Compares 5- and 6-digit numbers and justifies the comparison using place value."
What the learner knows

Knowledge Outcome

A concept that must be held for the performance to be possible. Reusable across many outcomes.

"Because each place is worth ten times the next, a difference in a higher place outranks any difference below it."

Two tests that hold up at primary level

"Can you grade it by watching?" is too blunt for ages 8–11, where nearly everything is watchable. These two discriminate better:

  • Can you be wrong about it? A Knowledge Outcome can be sincerely, falsely believed — a child can hold "the longer number is the bigger number" as a genuine conviction. A Performance Outcome has no false version; it is done or it is not.
  • Does it survive without the child? "Each place is worth ten times the one to its right" is true about the number system whether or not anyone is learning it. "Compares two 3-digit numbers" is a statement about a learner.

One working rule follows from this: every Procedure-type concept must carry its because. Without a rationale it is a method, and methods belong on the performance side.

A developmental caveat. At the Preparatory stage most mathematical knowledge is enactive — a child knows what a half is by making one. The gap between doing and knowing is genuinely narrower here than it will be at Secondary, where freestanding propositions dominate. Expect the two sides to separate more visibly as the spine extends upward.
§ 5

The data model

Every tab in the workbook is a normalised table designed to import directly into the database behind the application. Text is never duplicated across tables — relationships are carried by stable IDs.

ID convention

IDs are readable on sight, so a row can be traced without a join: LT-<competency>, PT-<competency>-T<n>, PO-<competency>-T<n>-<level>, KT-<competency>-K<n>, KO-<competency>-K<n>-<seq>.

Each concept also carries a type — Concept, Representation, Notation, Procedure, Property or Application — which tells the generation layer what kind of material to produce from it, and an optional common misconception, which becomes a distractor.

The performance side is complete for all 19 Preparatory Mathematics competencies. The knowledge side is complete for CG-1 Numbers & Operations only — 22 knowledge threads and 109 concepts across its 52 performance outcomes, all sourced against the NCERT Maths Mela textbooks for Classes 3, 4 and 5.

Spine-vs-book gaps found while reading the textbooks. These are logged rather than patched — they are exactly what Layer 2 exists to surface:
  • NCF expects number names for "very large numbers"; NCERT Classes 3–5 stop at five digits, with no lakh or crore.
  • NCF names fraction-as-division explicitly; no book in Classes 3–5 teaches it directly.
  • NCF names square, cube, triangular, powers-of-2 and Virahanka–Fibonacci numbers; none appear in Classes 3–5 — though the books do cover factors, multiples, primes and divisibility, which NCF does not name.

Open decisions

  • Grain. CG-1 runs 11–14 outcomes per competency against NCF's own illustration of 10. Target is roughly 8 outcomes and 12 concepts per competency.
  • Scope as a parameter. Number range (3-digit → 6-digit) is currently escalated across levels, which inflates the grid. It should be an attribute — and it is precisely what Layer 2 will consume.
  • Catalogue ownership. Move concepts from competency-scoped to a stage-wide shared catalogue, mapped at outcome level with competency derived.
  • Rationale rule. 14 of 18 Procedure concepts currently state a method with no because. Rewriting them as reusable ideas merges most away rather than deleting them.
