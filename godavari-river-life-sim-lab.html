Why?

Why do different lives live in different parts of the river?

A river is not the same all the way. In the hills its water is cold, fast and clear. On the plains it is warm, wide and slow. Behind a dam it stands still like a lake. Near the sea it mixes with salty water. Each kind of water, and each kind of bank, suits different plants and animals.

Why do people live near rivers?

People need water to drink, to grow crops, to wash and to catch fish. That is why so many villages, farms and towns are built along the Godavari. People are one of the lives the river supports.

Why do animals go away when the water gets dirty?

Dirty water holds less air and blocks sunlight, so fish, frogs, crabs and otters cannot live in it. When they go, the kingfisher, the fish eagle and the fisherman lose their food too. Every life on the river is linked to the others.

Why do you see different lives in different seasons?

In the monsoon the river fills up and turns muddy, and frogs and fish lay their eggs. In summer the water drops and some animals hide in cool mud. In winter, birds fly in from far-away cold lands, and sea turtles come to the beaches near the delta to lay eggs.

What does a dam change?

Dams like Sriram Sagar and Polavaram store water for farms and towns. But the lake behind a dam is still water, so the lives there are different, and fish that swim up and down the river find the wall hard to cross.

Try this. Go to the mangrove delta and slowly make the water dirty. Who leaves first? Who is left at the end? Now make it clean again and watch them come back.
